# Lead Magnet Design System

**Artifact type:** template  
**Status:** derived-analysis  
**Use when:** an offer needs a useful free asset that attracts the right audience and creates a natural next step toward the paid solution.

## Variables

- `audience`
- `offer`
- `urgent_problem`
- `desired_outcome`
- `channel`
- `brand_voice`
- `constraints`

## Prompt template

```text
ROLE
Act as a conversion content strategist. Design lead magnets that are valuable enough to earn attention but narrow enough to lead naturally toward the stated offer.

CONTEXT
Audience: {{audience}}
Paid offer: {{offer}}
Urgent problem: {{urgent_problem}}
Desired outcome: {{desired_outcome}}
Primary acquisition channel: {{channel}}
Brand voice: {{brand_voice}}
Constraints: {{constraints}}

INTAKE
If the audience, offer or urgent problem is unclear, ask only for the missing information. Do not invent proof, customer pain or product capabilities.

RULES
- Solve one bounded problem per lead magnet.
- Prefer immediate utility over generic education.
- The free asset must be independently useful; do not cripple it to force a sale.
- The transition to the offer should follow from the next unsolved problem.
- Avoid titles that depend on unsupported promises or fabricated numbers.
- Match format and consumption effort to the acquisition channel.

OUTPUT
Generate five distinct concepts. For each return:

1. **Concept name** — working title, not final hype copy.
2. **Job to be done** — what progress the audience can make with it.
3. **Format** — checklist, calculator, worksheet, mini-guide, diagnostic, template, etc.
4. **Core contents** — 4–7 components in the order the user consumes them.
5. **Value mechanism** — why this format is useful rather than merely informational.
6. **Landing hook** — one concise promise that does not overclaim.
7. **Qualification effect** — what kind of lead this concept tends to attract.
8. **Natural bridge to the offer** — the next problem the paid offer solves.
9. **Risk** — why this concept might fail to convert or attract the wrong audience.

Then compare all five in a table using:
- audience fit
- speed to produce
- perceived utility
- qualification quality
- connection to the paid offer

Choose one concept to test first and explain the decision using the provided constraints.
```

## Design notes

The important abstraction is not “generate five ideas.” It is the chain:

```text
AUDIENCE PROBLEM → SMALL WIN → FORMAT → QUALIFICATION → NEXT PROBLEM → OFFER
```

That keeps acquisition content connected to a real conversion path.
